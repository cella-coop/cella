# 7. Small Government

Bodies with 5-50 members. Direct participation, often part-time or volunteer representatives. Strong public transparency requirements.

## 7.1 Municipal Councils

Local government legislative bodies — city, town, or village councils.

**Primary modules:** Governance | **Related:** [§8.1 Regional assemblies](#81-regional-assemblies) (same model, larger), [§7.2 Citizen assemblies](#72-citizen-assemblies) (advisory bodies feeding into council)

### Context

| Dimension | Typical Range |
|-----------|---------------|
| Council size | 5-50 elected members |
| Committee structure | 3-10 standing committees |
| Meeting frequency | Bi-weekly to monthly |
| Public transparency | Legally mandated |

### Governance

- Elected representatives (direct or proportional)
- Mayor/president + council structure
- Standing committees for policy areas (finance, planning, social services)
- Formal procedures (standing orders, recorded votes)
- Public meetings with citizen input periods

### Financial Context

No profit distribution or member capital accounts. Financial needs focus on budget deliberation and amendment tracking, expenditure monitoring, capital project oversight, and public transparency.

### Workflows

**Legislative cycle:**

1. Motion/proposal introduced (by executive, committee, or member)
2. First reading and referral to committee
3. Committee review: evidence, public input, deliberation
4. Committee report with recommendations
5. Second reading: debate and amendments
6. Vote on amendments, then substantive motion
7. Third reading (if applicable) and final vote
8. Implementation assigned; progress tracked
9. Public record updated and searchable

### Pain Points

| Pain Point | Severity |
|------------|----------|
| Complex meeting packets (hundreds of pages as PDFs); poor navigation and version tracking | High |
| What was decided, when, by whom, with what vote — often poorly documented | High |
| Tracking changes to motions/budgets through multiple iterations | Medium |
| Work happening in parallel across committees; handoffs to full body | Medium |
| Legal transparency requirements met with static PDFs and poor search | Medium |

**Current tools:** Granicus/Legistar for meeting management (expensive, inflexible, US-focused), or just Word/PDF and email.

### Individual Personas

**Council Clerk / Secretary** — Preparing agendas and document packets, recording minutes and votes, maintaining official records, procedural compliance, public records requests. Drowning in paper and PDFs; version control nightmare with amendments.

**Elected Representative** — Reviewing proposals, committee participation, deliberation and voting, constituent communication. 200-page meeting packets as PDFs; hard to track changes; own voting record not easily accessible.

### References

- [Granicus](https://granicus.com/) — Dominant US platform for government meeting management and citizen engagement; expensive, inflexible, US-focused. Represents the incumbent Seedling would need to differentiate from
- [Decidim](https://decidim.org/) — Open-source participatory democracy platform used by 40+ cities; Barcelona, Helsinki, Mexico City. Strong on participation, weaker on legislative workflow
- [mySociety](https://www.mysociety.org) — UK civic tech org behind TheyWorkForYou and FixMyStreet; publishes Open Council Data Standards for transparency

## 7.2 Citizen Assemblies

Deliberative bodies convened for policy input, typically using sortition (random selection) or open participation. Often temporary or periodic.

**Primary modules:** Governance | **Related:** [§7.1 Municipal councils](#71-municipal-councils) (the body assemblies advise), [§8.2 Participatory budgeting](#82-participatory-budgeting-bodies) (similar citizen engagement, budget focus)

### Context

| Dimension | Typical Range |
|-----------|---------------|
| Participants | 15-50 (sortition) or open |
| Duration | Days to months |
| Output | Recommendations to elected body |

### Governance

- Facilitated deliberation (professional facilitators)
- Structured process: learning phase → deliberation → recommendations
- No voting power — advisory to elected bodies
- Emphasis on informed, balanced discussion

### Workflows

**Deliberative process:**

1. Assembly convened: participants selected by sortition or open call
2. Learning phase: expert presentations, site visits, evidence packs
3. Small-group deliberation on key questions
4. Plenary sessions to share group findings
5. Proposal development: participants draft recommendations
6. Internal voting on recommendations (advisory, not binding)
7. Recommendations presented to commissioning body (council, government)
8. Public report published with full provenance

### Pain Points

| Pain Point | Severity |
|------------|----------|
| Managing evidence and background materials for participants | High |
| Tracking deliberation across multiple sessions | Medium |
| Publishing recommendations with clear provenance | Medium |

**Current tools:** Ad-hoc — shared documents, presentation slides, paper-based deliberation tools. Some use Decidim or custom platforms for larger assemblies.

### Individual Personas

**Assembly Facilitator** — Designs and runs deliberation process. Needs structured session management, evidence presentation, recommendation drafting tools.

**Assembly Participant** — Reviews materials, deliberates, contributes to recommendations. Needs accessible materials, clear process guidance, ability to track what was discussed.

### References

- [OECD: Innovative Citizen Participation and New Democratic Institutions](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/innovative-citizen-participation-and-new-democratic-institutions_339306da-en.html) — Definitive study: 733 assemblies documented 1979-2023; criteria, outcomes, and design principles
- [Sortition Foundation](https://www.sortitionfoundation.org/what) — UK organization promoting sortition; practical guidance on running citizen assemblies
- [Irish Citizens' Assembly](https://www.citizensassembly.ie) — 99 randomly selected citizens deliberated on constitutional questions (2016-2018); led to Ireland's abortion referendum. Well-documented model
- [Convention Citoyenne pour le Climat](https://www.conventioncitoyennepourleclimat.fr) — 150 randomly selected French citizens produced 149 climate policy proposals (2019-2020); national-scale sortition example
